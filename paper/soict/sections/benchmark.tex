\section{Setting and Annotations}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\paragraph{Corpus and existing pipeline.}
We use 873 videos from the Ho Chi Minh City AI Challenge 2025 collection, sampled at one frame per second into 470{,}804 frames.
The collection is dominated by one cooking series that contributes 498 videos (57\%) recorded in the same studio.
Each frame has a SigLIP2 embedding~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2} (\texttt{siglip2-base-patch16-224}), a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct caption~\cite{qwen2025qwen3vl}, Florence-2-base-ft OCR~\cite{xiao2024florence2}, and organizer-provided objects.
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B~\cite{shi2026qwen3asr} supplies timestamped speech transcripts.
For ordered events $E_1,\dots,E_M$, frame scores combine SigLIP2 image--text similarity, BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3} similarity to context text built from caption, OCR, and objects, separate BGE-M3 ASR similarity, and BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25} over captions, OCR, ASR, and titles.
ASR evidence is projected onto its transcript intervals rather than treated as a frame description.

\paragraph{Score fusion.}
For each event, component scores are clipped at their 5th percentile over supported frames and rescaled to $[0,1]$, without clipping the upper tail.
With calibrated score $\hat s_{im}$, reliability $r_{im}$, and coverage $c_m(f)$, fusion gives
\begin{equation}
S_v[i,f]=\frac{\sum_m c_m(f)w_{im}r_{im}\hat s_{im}(f)}{\sum_m c_m(f)w_{im}r_{im}}.
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
Unavailable components are omitted from both sums; if no component contributes, the calibrated visual score is used.
BM25 reliability is 1 when a match exists and 0 otherwise.
Dense reliability is $z/(1+z)$, where
\[
z=\frac{\max(\bar{s}_{\mathrm{tail}}-s_{50},0)}{\mathrm{IQR}(s)+10^{-6}}.
\]
Statistics use supported raw scores: $s_{50}$ is their median and $\bar{s}_{\mathrm{tail}}$ averages the top 1\%, capped at 128 scores.
Base weights are 0.35 each for visual and context similarity, 0.08 for dense ASR, and 0.10, 0.04, 0.06, and 0.02 for caption, OCR, ASR, and title BM25.
Visual cues multiply visual/context weights by 1.4, speech cues multiply ASR weights by 5, and on-screen-text cues multiply OCR BM25 by 3.
These competition-development settings remain fixed throughout the study.

\paragraph{Ordered decoding.}
A monotone DP selects $f_1<\dots<f_M$ to maximize
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^{M} S_v[i,f_i]-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}(t_{f_{i+1}}-t_{f_i}),
\qquad \lambda=10^{-5}\ \mathrm{ms}^{-1}.
\label{eq:decoding}
\end{equation}
Video selection uses the highest-scoring path.
For failure prediction, we rank up to five paths per video by score, separated by at least 30\,s at the final event; the top-100 path list retains repeated videos.

\paragraph{Per-event intervals.}
We annotated 40 queries from the three preliminary-round query sets with a ground-truth time interval for every event, 113 events in total: 13 KIS queries from the first set, 7 and 15 KIS queries from the second and third sets, and 5 TRAKE queries.
Selection used authors' judgment of whether query text describes at least two visible moments.
Three multi-moment queries were initially omitted: a duplicate, one without a matching submitted video, and one not inspected then.
All event-level analyses retain the frozen 40-query cohort despite later annotation work; the release includes its manifest, annotations, and the 30 excluded query IDs.
Event texts are split from the original query wording so that each describes one visible moment.
Intervals were drafted from 1-fps keyframes around the known answer and then reviewed by an author on a page showing the keyframe strip and a link to the source video; the review changed 74 of 113 intervals (median boundary shift 2\,s, 14 by more than 5\,s) and no event text.
A predicted frame is correct if its timestamp lies within the interval widened by one second on each side, which absorbs the one-second sampling period.
Annotated intervals have a median length of 3\,s (interquartile range 2--5\,s), so the tolerance is not negligible; with zero tolerance the initial whole-sequence correctness of Sect.~\ref{sec:diagnosis} drops from 25\% to 20\% and the H@5 of Sect.~\ref{sec:correction} from 62.5\% to 55\%, and with 2\,s and 3\,s it is 27.5\%/62.5\% and 30\%/65\%.
We report whole-sequence correctness (all event timestamps inside their intervals) and event accuracy.
Ordered DP outputs are chronological by construction; order validity for the unconstrained comparisons is discussed separately in Sect.~\ref{sec:correction}.

\paragraph{Video-level answers.}
For failure prediction we only need the correct video.
We use all 70 KIS and TRAKE queries of the three sets (65 KIS, 5 TRAKE).
Answers for all three sets come from our competition submissions that the organizers scored as correct.
The first set names one video per submission; the other sets list several, so we visually confirm the first-ranked video.
Acceptance does not independently validate that choice; results are therefore reported per set.

\paragraph{Event texts.}
For the 40 annotated queries, events are the annotated texts, so that the audit isolates retrieval and alignment from query decomposition.
For the 70-query experiment we use a deterministic split without an LLM: the \texttt{E}$k$ lines of TRAKE queries and the sentences of KIS queries.
