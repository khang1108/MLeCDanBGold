\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Implications.}
The mechanisms address different failures but are evaluated separately.
A warning could prompt query reformulation or inspection of the next-ranked videos; an unflagged result still needs confirmation, since 5 of 24 are wrong with the fixed signal and 13 of 29 with training-only selection.
Confirmed videos can then undergo local event repair.
Within-video ranking remains a promising target: correct frames appear in the top 20 for 76\% of events but first for only 35\%.
The tested temporal variants do not close this gap; richer models remain untested.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The study covers 40 event-labelled and 70 video-labelled queries from one corpus and pipeline; confidence intervals are wide and generalization is untested.
Selection and annotation rely on authors' judgment without independent agreement; multi-candidate submission acceptance does not independently validate our selected video labels.
Correction assumes the correct video, error-free feedback, and oracle stopping, including uninspected events.
Presentations are not measured human effort, and one millisecond is decoder time only.
The 70\% correct-video result corresponds to 35\% when wrong videos count as failures.
Held-out thresholds, training-only signal selection, and sensitivity analyses reduce selection bias, but all results still come from the same query collection.
Annotated or deterministic event splits do not test an LLM planner.
Human usability, warning responses, and combined routing remain untested.
