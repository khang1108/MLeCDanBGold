\section{Exploratory Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Experimental Setup.}
We evaluated SHI against a baseline control interface over identical indexed video
collections, models, and retrieval settings. The control reflects standard competition
systems: supporting narrative search, multimodal ranking, and keyframe browsing, but
treating decomposition and temporal alignment as internal black-box procedures. In
contrast, SHI enables pre-retrieval Query Hypothesis revision and post-retrieval
EventTrail steering. We evaluated ten multi-event KIS queries with verified target
videos and intervals; a hit requires submitting within the ground-truth interval.

\paragraph{Results and Analysis.}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:kis-benchmark}, both interfaces produced identical
first-pass rankings (7/10 target videos in top-5, 60\% hit rate). Subsequent
interaction resolved two failure modes. First, for one query retrieved at rank 3,
visual ambiguity caused the initial decoder to select a false-positive frame;
EventTrail allowed the operator to anchor the true occurrence, re-decoding the
path in under 100\,ms from the Evidence Snapshot without corpus re-querying.
Second, for two queries initially outside the top-20 due to suboptimal LLM
segmentation, restructuring the Query Hypothesis elevated the target video into
top ranks. One query failed due to severe visual occlusion and absent speech cues.
Overall, dual-stage steering increased timestamp accuracy from 60\% to 90\%,
confirming that intermediate hypothesis intervention mitigates cascade errors.
