\begin{abstract}
Finding the correct video does not ensure that every event in a multi-event query is placed at the correct moment.
We present an empirical reliability study that separates video-selection errors from within-video event-alignment errors in a training-free multimodal retrieval pipeline with ordered dynamic programming (DP).
On the Ho Chi Minh City AI Challenge collection of 873 videos, we freeze per-event annotations for 40 queries (113 events) and use video-level answers for 70 queries.
Even with the correct video given, only 25\% of queries have all events placed correctly.
The relative margin between the top video and the next distinct video discriminates correct from wrong first passes with AUROC 0.85; selecting the signal on training query sets gives held-out AUROCs of 0.78--0.85.
With the correct video given, simulated oracle feedback raises whole-sequence correctness from 25\% to 70\% within five actions with eight candidates per event, versus 47.5\% without DP.
Most of this difference comes from the DP starting path and order constraints, while propagation to untouched events contributes little.
When wrong first-pass videos count as failures, correctness rises from 10\% to 35\%.
The study identifies within-video frame ranking as a bottleneck of this pipeline and quantifies correction headroom under oracle feedback; it does not establish the effectiveness of a combined routing policy or human interaction.
\keywords{Multi-Event Video Retrieval \and Temporal Alignment \and Query Performance Prediction \and Interactive Correction}
\end{abstract}
