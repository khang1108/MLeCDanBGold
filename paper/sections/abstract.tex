\begin{abstract}
Large language models can decompose complex video-search narratives into
ordered events, but errors in event boundaries or temporal relations may
affect downstream retrieval. In multi-event search, such errors are difficult
to diagnose when both query interpretation and temporal alignment remain
internal to the retrieval pipeline. We present SHI, an interactive
video retrieval system with two interaction stages: pre-retrieval and
post-retrieval. In the pre-retrieval stage, the Query Hypothesis
exposes the LLM-generated decomposition as an editable ordered sequence of
events, allowing users to inspect and revise the search structure before
retrieval. In the post-retrieval stage, EventTrail exposes the decoded
temporal alignment of a candidate video and presents alternative event
occurrences as complete ordered paths rather than isolated frames. Users can
anchor accepted occurrences or exclude incorrect temporal regions, after which
the alignment is re-decoded under the resulting constraints. Because
re-decoding operates on retained retrieval evidence, these corrections can be
applied without repeating corpus-level multimodal retrieval. Together, the two
stages make both semantic decomposition and temporal alignment explicit
interaction objects in multi-event video search.

\end{abstract}
