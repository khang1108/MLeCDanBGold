\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Video Browser Showdown (VBS) evaluates interactive video retrieval systems
through live search tasks over large-scale video collections
~\cite{lokoc2021reign}.
In the multi-event Known-Item Search (KIS) setting considered in this work,
queries describe complex narratives comprising several temporally ordered visual
events~\cite{wu2021sql,heller2022temporalqueries}. Resolving such queries involves
two intermediate decisions: how the narrative is decomposed into searchable
events, and how those events are subsequently assigned to temporal occurrences
within a candidate video.

Large language models (LLMs) can transform unstructured narratives into
structured event sequences, but prior research shows that they still struggle
with temporal dependencies in complex task decomposition and sequential planning
~\cite{yuan2024tasklama,valmeekam2023planbench}.
In multi-event video search, an incorrect decomposition can propagate to
subsequent retrieval and temporal alignment stages. Simultaneously, evaluations
of VBS competitions demonstrate that search success depends on more than the
initial ranking alone~\cite{sauter2024performance}. Browsing and supplementary query
modalities contributed substantially to performance in a post-evaluation of VBS 2022
~\cite{schall2024interactive}, while VBS 2026 interaction logs show that users
predominantly reformulate queries and inspect candidate results
~\cite{jackl2026interaction}. However, current interactive video retrieval systems
typically treat query decomposition and temporal alignment as internal, black-box
processes~\cite{amato2024visione,lokoc2024prak,gia2025niiuit,khan2026temporalqueries},
leaving users unable to diagnose or correct intermediate errors.

To address this limitation, we present SHI, an interactive multimodal
video retrieval system with complementary pre-retrieval and post-retrieval
interaction. Before retrieval, the Query Hypothesis exposes the
LLM-generated event decomposition as an editable ordered sequence, allowing
users to inspect semantic boundaries, restructure event order, and bind
reference images. After retrieval, EventTrail exposes the decoded
temporal alignment of candidate videos and presents alternative event
occurrences as complete ordered paths rather than isolated keyframes. Users can
anchor valid occurrences or exclude false-positive temporal regions, after which
SHI re-decodes the path from retained multimodal evidence snapshots without
repeating corpus-level retrieval. In exploratory evaluations on multi-event KIS
queries, this dual-stage interaction increased valid timestamp accuracy from
60\% to 90\%, demonstrating that inspecting and correcting intermediate
hypotheses effectively recovers from early-stage cascade errors.
