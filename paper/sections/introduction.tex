\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Video Browser Showdown (VBS) evaluates interactive video retrieval systems
through live search tasks, among which multi-event Known-Item Search (KIS)
requires an operator to locate a video described by a sequence of temporally
ordered events~\cite{vbs2027cfp}. Compared with single-event retrieval, this
setting introduces two additional sources of uncertainty: the narrative must
first be decomposed into suitable retrieval events, and the retrieved evidence
must then be aligned to occurrences that satisfy the intended temporal order.
Errors in either stage can affect candidate ranking and complicate subsequent
inspection.

LLMs provide a convenient way to convert unstructured narratives into structured
event sequences. Prior work on complex task understanding shows that LLMs can
identify individual task steps effectively, while still exhibiting difficulty in
predicting temporal dependencies between those steps~\cite{yuan2024tasklama}. A
plausible-looking decomposition may therefore still contain event boundaries or
ordering assumptions that do not match the operator's intended search strategy,
and executing it directly propagates these errors to multimodal scoring and
temporal alignment. We therefore treat the LLM output as a search hypothesis
rather than a fixed interpretation, and expose it for inspection and editing
before retrieval.

Interaction has repeatedly been identified as an important component of VBS
search performance: a post-evaluation of VBS 2022 found that strong CLIP-based
text retrieval must be complemented by browsing and additional query
modalities~\cite{schall2024interactive}, and interaction logs from VBS 2026 show
heavy reliance on iterative text-query reformulation coupled with result-set
inspection~\cite{jackl2026interaction}. Existing systems provide rich mechanisms
for query formulation, browsing, and relevance
feedback~\cite{amato2024visione,lokoc2024prak}; for multi-event search, however,
the decoded temporal alignment of a candidate is typically not exposed as an
object that the operator can directly inspect and
modify~\cite{amato2024visione,lokoc2024prak,sharma2025exquisitor,heller2022temporalqueries,khan2026temporalqueries}.
When several events are matched correctly but one occurrence is temporally
misaligned, the operator may therefore need to reformulate the query or continue
browsing despite already having useful retrieval evidence.

To address these two interaction points, we present SHI
(Fig.~\ref{fig:system-ui}), an interactive multimodal video retrieval system for
multi-event KIS. SHI exposes the LLM-generated event decomposition as an editable
structured plan before retrieval, and the decoded temporal alignment of a
candidate video as an ordered event path after retrieval. Constraints derived
from operator actions are applied by re-decoding the alignment from retained
retrieval evidence, without repeating corpus-level retrieval.
Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the retrieval pipeline, and
Section~\ref{sec:interactive-hypothesis-correction} the two interaction
mechanisms.
