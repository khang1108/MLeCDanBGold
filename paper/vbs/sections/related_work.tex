\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Interactive video retrieval systems developed for the Video Browser Showdown
(VBS) combine multimodal retrieval with mechanisms for query formulation,
iterative refinement, and result exploration: textual, visual-example, and
sketch-based queries~\cite{amato2024visione}, search and filtering over video
data services~\cite{lokoc2024prak}, and conversational search with user
relevance feedback~\cite{sharma2025exquisitor}. Evaluations of VBS systems have
repeatedly highlighted the importance of iterative query reformulation and
result inspection in addition to the quality of the initial
ranking~\cite{lokoc2023queryreformulation,schall2024interactive,jackl2026interaction}. 
These systems provide rich mechanisms for modifying
queries and exploring retrieved results, but the decomposition of a narrative
request into an ordered sequence of retrieval events is generally not exposed
as an editable intermediate representation~\cite{amato2024visione,lokoc2024prak,sharma2025exquisitor}. SHI addresses this interaction
point through the Query Hypothesis, which presents the event
decomposition explicitly and allows operators to modify its structure before
retrieval.

Temporal structure has also been incorporated into interactive video retrieval
through query specification, retrieval, and feedback over candidate intervals:
SQL-like templates for temporal query evolution~\cite{wu2021sql}, multimodal
queries with temporal context~\cite{heller2022temporalqueries}, sequence-chain
retrieval for temporal queries~\cite{khan2026temporalqueries}, and user
feedback over retrieved temporal intervals~\cite{ma2022ivcmr}.
SHI focuses on a complementary interaction point: the decoded multi-event
alignment of an individual candidate video. Its EventTrail exposes this
alignment as an ordered path, allows operators to anchor or reject individual
event occurrences, and re-decodes the path under the resulting temporal
constraints using retained retrieval evidence.
